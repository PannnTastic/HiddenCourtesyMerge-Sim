\section{Benchmark and Methods}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\subsection{Simulation Environment and Courtesy Injection}
We use the \texttt{merge-v0} environment from highway-env
\citep{leurent_highway_env} (v1.11): a two-lane main road with a single-lane
on-ramp, capped at 60 policy steps ($\Delta t{=}0.2$\,s, 12\,s total).
At each episode reset, a ramp vehicle is selected as the merge vehicle and
assigned a hidden courtesy type $m \in \{\text{cooperative},
\text{non-cooperative}\}$ sampled with equal probability.
Six IDM/MOBIL parameters are drawn uniformly from courtesy-specific ranges
(Table~\ref{tab:courtesy_params}) and injected as instance attributes,
shadowing class-level defaults without any per-step override or teleportation.
Cooperative vehicles slow to create gaps; non-cooperative vehicles maintain
high speed and small headways.
A 3{,}000-episode dataset (seed 7, 70/15/15 split by episode index) is
generated using \texttt{belief\_policy} as the ego; this introduces a survivorship bias---high-risk states are under-represented
relative to a random ego---that does not affect the seed-17 matched policy
evaluation reported in \S\ref{sec:results}, but researchers training
learning-based planners or encoders on the released dataset should account
for this trajectory-distribution bias.
The dataset confirms that non-cooperative episodes produce higher collision
rates, more close-call events, and shorter minimum TTC on all five monotone
risk-ordering checks (Table~\ref{tab:risk_by_courtesy}).
26.0\% of episodes (778/3{,}000) contain no in-window steps because the merge
vehicle completes its lane change before $d_t$ enters $(2,40)$\,m; for these
episodes the belief remains at $b_0$ throughout and the policy effectively
reduces to the rule baseline.
Retaining these episodes preserves the ecological distribution of merge
interactions at the benchmark traffic density rather than conditioning on
interactions of a specific type.

\subsection{POMDP Specification}
\label{sec:pomdp}
The problem is formalised as an abstracted hidden-parameter POMDP interface.
We write the tuple as
\[
  \mathcal{M}{=}(S,A,O,T,Z,R,\gamma,b_0).
\]
The compact tuple exposes the merge vehicle, ego vehicle, and binary courtesy
mode used by the benchmark policies; the full highway-env simulator state
remains inside the transition kernel.
The state decomposes into an observable component and a single hidden
component:
\[
  s_t = \bigl(\underbrace{x_e,\,v_e,\,\ell_e,\,x_m,\,v_m,\,\ell_m,\,
  d_t,\,\Delta v_t}_{\text{observable}},\;
  \underbrace{m}_{\text{hidden}}\bigr).
\]
The observable component comprises the longitudinal position $x_e$\,(m)
and speed $v_e$\,(m/s) of the ego vehicle, together with its lane index
$\ell_e$ (fixed to the main road throughout the episode); the longitudinal
position $x_m$\,(m) and speed $v_m$\,(m/s) of the merge vehicle, together
with its lane index $\ell_m$ (on-ramp prior to merge, main road thereafter);
the signed longitudinal gap $d_t{=}x_m{-}x_e$\,(m) (both positions measured
along the common longitudinal road axis), which is negative when
the merge vehicle trails the ego; and the relative speed
$\Delta v_t{=}v_m{-}v_e$\,(m/s), which is positive when the merge vehicle
is closing.
The quantities $d_t$ and $\Delta v_t$ are deterministic functions of
$(x_e,v_e,x_m,v_m)$ but are included explicitly in $s_t$ because the
observation model and action rules consume them directly.
The hidden component $m\in\{\text{cooperative},\,\text{non-cooperative}\}$
is the courtesy type, sampled once at episode start and fixed for the entire
episode: IDM/MOBIL parameters are injected as instance attributes at reset
and are not modified thereafter, so $P(m_{t+1}{=}m_t){=}1$ holds by
construction of the simulator, not as a modelling assumption.
The transition function factorises as
$T(s'\!\mid\!s,a) = \delta(m'{=}m)\cdot T_{\!\text{env}}(s'_{\text{obs}}\mid s_{\text{obs}},a)$,
where $T_{\!\text{env}}$ is the opaque highway-env simulator dynamics and
$\delta(m'{=}m)$ encodes the static hidden state.
Surrounding traffic, downstream lane availability, and road geometry are
represented inside $T_{\!\text{env}}$ but are not enumerated in this compact
tuple; richer explicit state representations are left for future benchmark
variants.
The heuristic policies are memoryless and do not use $\gamma$; the POMCP
solver uses $\gamma{=}0.98$ (see \S\ref{sec:pomcp_policy}).
The ego selects from five discrete meta-actions (LANE\_LEFT, IDLE,
LANE\_RIGHT, FASTER, SLOWER).
In \texttt{merge-v0}'s single-merge-lane geometry the ego occupies the
main road throughout, making LANE\_LEFT, LANE\_RIGHT, and FASTER
infeasible or counter-productive during the merge interaction; all evaluated
non-random policies restrict to $\{\text{IDLE},\text{SLOWER}\}$.
The evaluation reward is the highway-env reward minus a close-call penalty,
\[
  R_t = r^{\mathrm{env}}_t - 0.25\,
  \mathbf{1}[\mathrm{TTC}_t<3\mathrm{s}\ \lor\ d_t\in(0,15\mathrm{m})],
\]
where $r^{\mathrm{env}}_t \approx v_e/v_{\mathrm{ref}} \in [0,1]$ is the
highway-env speed reward ($v_{\mathrm{ref}}{=}30$\,m/s).
The coefficient 0.25 was selected by manual inspection on the training split
and is not claimed to be optimal.
Replaying stored per-step rewards under alternate close-call weights confirms
that the policy reward ordering is stable for $w\in\{0.10,0.25,0.50,1.00\}$
(Table~\ref{tab:penalty_ablation}), although closed-loop policies were not
rerun under alternate reward definitions.
POMCP uses a separate planning reward described below, creating a deliberate
but disclosed planning--evaluation mismatch.
The observation space is $O{=}\mathbb{R}^3$; each observation
$o_t{=}(o_{t,\text{ard}},\,o_{t,\text{mvs}},\,o_{t,\text{mva}})$ is active
inside the interaction window ($2\,\text{m}{<}d_t{<}40\,\text{m}$) and null
outside it, in which case the belief is carried forward unchanged.
The observation likelihood is a product of univariate Gaussians over these
three features:
\[
  Z(o_t \mid m)
  = \prod_{i \in \{\text{ard},\,\text{mvs},\,\text{mva}\}}
    \mathcal{N}(o_{t,i};\,\mu_{m,i},\,\sigma_{m,i}^2).
\]
The Bayesian belief is updated as
\begin{align}
  \tilde{b}_{t+1}(m) &= \eta \cdot Z(o_t \mid m) \cdot b_t(m), \label{eq:bayes}\\
  b_{t+1} &= (1-\lambda)\,\tilde{b}_{t+1} + \lambda\,b_0, \label{eq:reg}
\end{align}
where $\eta$ is the normalisation constant ensuring that $\tilde{b}_{t+1}$ sums
to one over $m$,
and $\lambda{=}0.08$ shrinks the posterior toward the uniform prior
$b_0{=}[0.5,\,0.5]$ to mitigate overconfidence from temporally correlated
observations.
The value $\lambda{=}0.08$ was selected by grid search over
$\{0.00,0.02,0.04,0.08,0.12,0.16,0.20\}$ on the train and validation splits
(2{,}550 episodes; test split withheld), targeting final-step belief accuracy
(the metric most relevant to downstream policy quality;
Table~\ref{tab:lambda_ablation}).
It achieves 93.8\% final-step accuracy, within $0.1$\,pp of the peak at
$\lambda{=}0.12$ (93.9\%), while maintaining lower expected calibration
error ($\text{ECE}{=}0.063$) than all smaller values.
The heuristic policies are not optimal POMDP solvers; POMCP is an approximate
online planner evaluated as a planning baseline.

\subsection{Observation Model}
\label{sec:obs_model}
The three active features are: \textbf{absolute relative distance}
(\emph{ard}, m), reflecting gap-acceptance behaviour (class separation
$|\Delta\mu|/\bar\sigma{=}0.48$); \textbf{merge vehicle speed}
(\emph{mvs}, m/s), directly tracking the courtesy-injected target speed
(separation 1.32); and \textbf{merge vehicle acceleration}
(\emph{mva}, m/s$^2$), capturing sustained cooperative deceleration
($\mu_\text{coop}{=}-0.938$) vs.\ near-zero non-cooperative mean
($\mu_\text{nc}{=}-0.045$, separation 0.81).
Two candidate features were excluded using pre-specified criteria: urgency
(\emph{urg}) for weak class separation ($|\Delta\mu|/\bar\sigma{<}0.30$;
observed 0.14), and relative speed (\emph{rs}) for near-perfect collinearity
with \emph{mvs} (Pearson $r{=}0.991$), which violates the product-of-Gaussians
independence assumption (Table~\ref{tab:feature_corr}).

Gaussian parameters $(\mu_{m,i},\sigma_{m,i})$ are calibrated from 60
simulator rollouts per courtesy mode at seed 999, yielding 290 cooperative
and 1{,}519 non-cooperative in-window steps (5.2:1 imbalance;
Table~\ref{tab:obs_model_params}).
The imbalance arises because cooperative vehicles spend fewer steps in the
interaction window; it inflates the standard error of cooperative parameter
estimates by approximately $\sqrt{1519/290}{\approx}2.3{\times}$ relative to
non-cooperative estimates, and is noted as a limitation (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
Calibration (seed 999), dataset generation (seed 7), and policy evaluation
(seed 17) use deliberately disjoint global seeds to prevent cross-contamination
between observation-model fitting and downstream evaluation.
Per-feature minimum-$\sigma$ floors ($\sigma_\text{min}\in[0.05,2.0]$\,m
or m/s; Table~\ref{tab:obs_model_params}) prevent degenerate likelihoods.
The three-feature model achieves 98.3\% held-out step-level classification
accuracy relative to a balanced-class Bernoulli baseline
(NLL $0.168$ vs.\ $\ln 2{=}0.693$, a $4.1{\times}$ reduction;
ECE $0.063$ on the 3{,}000-episode dataset).
A feature ablation confirms that removing \emph{ard} reduces final-step
accuracy from 93.8\% to 87.3\%: despite borderline class separation
($|\Delta\mu|/\bar\sigma{=}0.48$), \emph{ard} provides an orthogonal
proximity signal that complements the velocity features \emph{mvs} and
\emph{mva}, and its removal is non-redundant
(Table~\ref{tab:obs_feature_ablation}).
An offline held-out ablation further shows that a full-covariance Gaussian
over \{\emph{ard}, \emph{mvs}, \emph{mva}\} improves filter metrics relative
to the canonical diagonal likelihood (Table~\ref{tab:covariance_ablation}):
$+3.3$\,pp step-level accuracy (82.8\%\,$\to$\,86.1\%) and $+5.5$\,pp
final-step accuracy (92.2\%\,$\to$\,97.7\%) on 7{,}275 held-out in-window steps.
The gain is driven primarily by the cooperative class, which exhibits substantial
within-class correlations (\emph{ard}--\emph{mvs}: $r{=}0.60$;
\emph{mvs}--\emph{mva}: $r{=}0.57$) that the diagonal model ignores;
the non-cooperative class shows near-zero within-class correlations.
We retain the diagonal model for the primary closed-loop evaluation to keep
the released policy artifacts fixed, but the full-covariance result identifies
a concrete modeling improvement for future benchmark users.

\subsection{Policy Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}
\paragraph{Heuristic policies.}
Four heuristic policies share a V-shaped caution rule mapping a belief
vector $b_t$ to an action in $\{\text{IDLE},\text{SLOWER}\}$.
The SLOWER probability in the moderate-TTC regime is
$p_\text{SLOWER}{=}0.735{+}0.185|2b_\text{coop}{-}1|$; in the low-urgency
regime it is $0.205{+}0.125|2b_\text{coop}{-}1|$
(all coefficients were determined by manual search on the training split,
targeting a balance between collision avoidance and throughput under the
V-shaped caution structure; we did not perform further automated optimisation).
Emergency overrides (TTC\,$<$\,3\,s or gap\,$<$\,8\,m) trigger SLOWER with
probability 0.98.
The four policies differ only in the belief passed to this function
(Table~\ref{tab:policy_defs}): \texttt{random\_policy} selects uniformly
from all five actions (unconditional lower bound); \texttt{rule\_policy}
fixes $b{=}[0.5,0.5]$ (isolates the value of belief estimation);
\texttt{belief\_policy} maintains a Bayesian belief via
\eqref{eq:bayes}--\eqref{eq:reg} (primary interest); and
\texttt{oracle\_policy} passes a one-hot belief at the true courtesy type
(upper bound for this action table only---not an optimal POMDP policy).

\paragraph{POMCP policy.}
\label{sec:pomcp_policy}
As a planning-based solver, we implement POMCP \citep{silver2010pomcp} with
UCT search over the binary-mode hidden-parameter POMDP.
Because $m$ is fixed per episode, each simulation samples a mode from the
external Bayesian belief and treats it as known for the full trajectory;
no particle propagation over $m$ is needed since $m$ is constant within an
episode by construction.
The generative model uses mean IDM parameters per courtesy type to propagate
$(d_t, v_{e,t}, v_{m,t})$ analytically.
The rollout heuristic selects SLOWER when $d{<}15$\,m or TTC\,$<$\,6\,s
and IDLE otherwise---a more conservative trigger than the heuristic
emergency override (TTC\,$<$\,3\,s or gap\,$<$\,8\,m).
Table~\ref{tab:pomcp_rollout_ablation} reports a rollout-isolation run using
the heuristic emergency trigger.
The planning reward is $v_e/30 - 100\,\mathbf{1}[d{<}1.5\,\text{m}]
- 1.0\,\mathbf{1}[\text{TTC}{<}3\,\text{s}]$; this uses a narrower
collision zone ($d{<}1.5$\,m) and differs from the environment evaluation
reward, introducing a planning--evaluation reward mismatch.
See limitations (\S\ref{sec:discussion}) for discussion of the remaining
planning--evaluation mismatch.
At each decision step, 100 UCT simulations run with horizon 10 steps
(2\,s) and $\gamma{=}0.98$; across timesteps the subtree rooted at the
executed action is retained with its visit counts, and all remaining branches
are discarded.
POMCP optimises discounted return ($\gamma{=}0.98$) while evaluation uses
undiscounted episode mean reward; this is a further acknowledged
planning--evaluation mismatch beyond the reward-function discrepancy noted above.
The belief is maintained by the same external Bayesian filter as
\texttt{belief\_policy}, ensuring a fair comparison.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}
All five policies are evaluated over $n{=}300$ episodes at global seed 17.
Each episode index maps to a fixed environment seed, hidden courtesy type,
traffic density, and observation noise shared by all policies (matched
design); action sampling uses separate per-policy RNG offsets to avoid
cross-contamination.
We report collision rate, mean reward, mean minimum TTC, and close-call rate
with bootstrap 95\% CIs (10{,}000 resamples) for binary metrics and
normal-approximation CIs for continuous metrics.
Paired binary comparisons use McNemar's test \citep{mcnemar1947note};
paired continuous comparisons use the paired $t$-test; Holm--Bonferroni
correction \citep{holm1979simple} is applied jointly across all
$\binom{5}{2}{\times}2{=}20$ pairwise comparisons and metrics in a single
family; Cohen's $d$ for continuous metrics is computed as
$\bar\delta/s_\delta$, the ratio of the mean to the standard deviation of
paired differences \citep{cohen1988statistical}, and Cohen's $h$ for binary
metrics uses the arcsine transformation.
