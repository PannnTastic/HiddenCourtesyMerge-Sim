\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{HiddenCourtesyMerge-Sim}, a controlled simulation
benchmark for belief-based planning under hidden driver courtesy in
highway merge scenarios.
The benchmark introduces a binary latent variable---driver courtesy type
(cooperative or non-cooperative)---injected via IDM/MOBIL parameter
modification at episode start without per-step overrides or vehicle
teleportation, providing ground-truth latent labels that are absent from
naturalistic datasets.
We provide (i)~a 3{,}000-episode dataset with balanced courtesy classes,
deterministic train/validation/test splits, and step-level motion and belief
columns; (ii)~a calibrated product-of-Gaussians observation model over three
selected features, achieving 98.3\% held-out step-level classification
accuracy (NLL $0.168$ vs.\ $\ln 2{=}0.693$), with the sequential Bayesian
filter reaching 83.6\% in-window and 93.8\% final-step belief accuracy on
the dataset; (iii)~an abstracted hidden-parameter POMDP interface with
Bayesian belief update; and (iv)~a five-policy comparative evaluation
(four heuristic baselines plus a POMCP planning solver) with matched scenarios,
bootstrapped 95\% confidence intervals, Holm--Bonferroni corrected paired
hypothesis tests, by-courtesy subgroup analysis, and a post-hoc power analysis.
Robustness checks stress-test the main claims against action-gain variation,
close-call reward weighting, POMCP rollout triggers, covariance structure in
the observation model, and paired McNemar discordant counts.

Across $n{=}300$ matched episodes, a POMCP planning solver (100 UCT
simulations per decision step) achieves the lowest collision rate of all
five policies (5.0\%; 95\% CI [2.7\%, 7.7\%]) and highest mean reward
(57.36), significantly outperforming all heuristic baselines, including the
oracle-heuristic baseline, after Holm--Bonferroni correction
(vs.\ oracle: McNemar $p_\text{adj}{=}0.014$, $|h|{=}0.204$;
reward $p_\text{adj}{=}0.0011$, $d{=}0.221$).
An oracle-heuristic policy with access to the true hidden courtesy type
significantly reduces collision rate relative to a belief-agnostic rule baseline
(10.3\% vs.\ 18.3\%; McNemar $\chi^2{=}11.02$, $p_\text{adj}{=}0.005$,
$|h|{=}0.230$) and improves mean reward (56.46 vs.\ 54.90;
$p_\text{adj}{=}0.00056$, $d{=}0.233$).
The Bayesian belief policy is ordered between the oracle-heuristic and rule
baselines (collision 15.0\%, reward 55.33) but does not significantly
outperform rule at $n{=}300$ ($p_\text{adj}{=}0.33$ for all metrics);
a power analysis indicates that $n{\approx}1{,}960$ episodes are needed for
80\% power.
A belief-gain sweep across five sensitivity values rules out
action-table expressiveness as the bottleneck, pointing to
early-window observation quality as the upstream constraint.
Random actions provide an unconditional lower bound
(87.0\% collision rate, $p_\text{adj}{<}0.001$ vs.\ rule).
A preliminary QMDP baseline was implemented but excluded from the primary
comparison after Q-table coverage failure (50\% state coverage) and a
34--35\% collision rate.

All results are specific to the described simulation and do not imply
real-world autonomous driving performance or realistic human courtesy behaviour.
An anonymized review repository will be provided during submission; the
de-anonymized dataset, code, and figures will be released upon acceptance to
support reproducible POMDP research on hidden-parameter driver modelling.
\end{abstract}
